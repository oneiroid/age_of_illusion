The Two-Phase Law

Deus ex Futuro, §2. Each dot is a world. Its row is its phase, and its horizontal position is the complexity it has built up in that phase. Its colour is its tension, from cool to hot. When tension crosses the threshold the world enters a short fast phase (ringed dots). It then either bifurcates up a row or falls back in a revolution. A world that falls below the first row is extinct, and a new world begins in its place.

Reading it

Management is the third path. It relaxes tension gradually, but the inflow of tension compounds within each phase, so management only postpones the crisis. With management raised, slow phases last longer, and worlds reach their crises with more complexity. That improves the odds of bifurcation, but no setting abolishes the fast phase.

Background suffering falls with each phase. Revolutions spike it far above what a bifurcation costs (§5).
